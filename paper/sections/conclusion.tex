\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

CMCA shows that controlled, seeded tool faults can be applied inside released coding-agent harnesses at both the service layer and the built-in tool layer, with conformance checks that tell which faults actually reach the model.
In a first measurement with one model, faults lowered success in every configuration, agents reported success for most failed faulted trials, and recovery consisted mostly of retries and cost more tokens and steps.
Differences between harness configurations in Faulted Success are descriptive: none survives multiple-comparison correction, and the study is not powered to detect differences below \cmcaTrackNFixedMddMin{} to \cmcaTrackSMddMax{} in Faulted Success.

The next steps follow from the limitations: more tasks, calibration that does not use a compared configuration, exposure-matched analyses on the native track, human validation of the outcome rules, verifiable delivery for truncation and drift, and runs with other models.
