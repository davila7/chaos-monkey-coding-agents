\subsection{RQ1: success under faults}

Table~\ref{tab:track-s} and Table~\ref{tab:track-n} give Faulted Success per configuration.
On the service track fx completes the most faulted trials (\cmcaTrackSLeaderSucceeded{} of \cmcaTrackSLeaderSent{}) and \cmcaTrackSLast{} the fewest (\cmcaTrackSLastSucceeded{}).
On the native track fxh completes the most (\cmcaTrackNFixedLeaderSucceeded{} of \cmcaTrackNFixedLeaderSent{}) and \cmcaTrackNFixedLast{} the fewest (\cmcaTrackNFixedLastSucceeded{}).
Clean pass$^1$ ranges from \cmcaTrackSCleanMin{} to \cmcaTrackSCleanMax{} on the service track and from \cmcaTrackNFixedCleanMin{} to \cmcaTrackNFixedCleanMax{} on the native track, so faults, not task difficulty, account for most of the failures.

These orderings are descriptive.
All Faulted Success intervals overlap, so each board has a single rank band.
No paired difference survives the Holm correction over the \cmcaTrackSPairs{} pairs of each board: the largest mean per-task difference is \cmcaTrackSMaxDifference{} on the service track (unadjusted $p = \cmcaTrackSMaxDifferenceP$, smallest adjusted $p = \cmcaTrackSMinPHolm$) and \cmcaTrackNFixedMaxDifference{} on the native track (smallest adjusted $p = \cmcaTrackNFixedMinPHolm$).
The minimum detectable difference at 80\% power is \cmcaTrackSMddMin{} to \cmcaTrackSMddMax{} on the service track and \cmcaTrackNFixedMddMin{} to \cmcaTrackNFixedMddMax{} on the native track, larger than every observed difference.
Detecting a 10-point difference with three attempts would need \cmcaTrackSTasksNeededMin{} to \cmcaTrackSTasksNeededMax{} tasks on the service track and \cmcaTrackNFixedTasksNeededMin{} to \cmcaTrackNFixedTasksNeededMax{} on the native track, depending on the pair.
The absence of a significant difference is therefore not evidence that the configurations are equally robust.

\paragraph{Fault exposure.}
The same profile does not give every configuration the same faults (Table~\ref{tab:exposure}).
On the service track the configurations receive similar numbers of applied faults per trial, because tool names and arguments are shared.
On the native track exposure differs widely: harnesses issue different numbers and kinds of tool calls, Codex reads files through the shell, and parity sets differ, so \cmcaTrackNFixedExposureMaxLabel{} receives \cmcaTrackNFixedExposureRatio{} times as many applied faults per trial as \cmcaTrackNFixedExposureMinLabel{}.
Native-track Faulted Success therefore mixes tolerance to faults with how much fault opportunity a harness creates; we report it end to end and do not normalize for exposure.

\subsection{RQ2: honesty}

Overclaiming is common in every configuration (Table~\ref{tab:honesty}).
Among failed trials that received a fault, the share whose status file reports success ranges from \cmcaTrackSOverclaimMin{} to \cmcaTrackSOverclaimMax{} on the service track and from \cmcaTrackNFixedOverclaimMin{} to \cmcaTrackNFixedOverclaimMax{} on the native track.
Claude Code has the lowest point estimate on the service track, but the denominators are small (\cmcaTrackNFixedFailedWithFaultMin{} to \cmcaTrackNFixedFailedWithFaultMax{} failed trials per row on the native track, \cmcaTrackSFailedWithFaultMin{} to \cmcaTrackSFailedWithFaultMax{} on the service track) and the intervals overlap.
Missing status files are rare and are counted separately.
Poison tokens reach the output in a small share of delivered stale-data events in every configuration; because the measure only detects the literal token, these rates are lower bounds on stale-data influence.
No configuration repeated a non-idempotent side effect after a false failure on the service track (Table~\ref{tab:outcomes}); targeted false failures do not apply on the native track.

\subsection{RQ3: recovery and its cost}

Table~\ref{tab:outcomes} classifies what the agent did within five steps of each applied fault.
On the service track agents mostly retry: \cmcaTrackSRetryShare{} of \cmcaTrackSEvents{} events are followed by a retry of the same or a modified call, and none is classified as a reroute.
On the native track the mix is broader: retries are \cmcaTrackNFixedRetryShare{} of \cmcaTrackNFixedEvents{} events, reroutes \cmcaTrackNFixedRerouteShare{}, and \cmcaTrackNFixedPassiveShare{} end in acknowledgment only, no visible reaction, or abandonment.
Recovery costs tokens and steps: the per-task median ratio of \code{moderate} to \code{clean} tokens is above 1 for every configuration, and higher on the service track.
These outcome labels come from deterministic rules that have not been checked against human labels, so they describe agent actions, not whether the action was the right one.

\subsection{Severity profiles}

Figure~\ref{fig:curves} plots pass$^1$ under the four profiles.
The profiles change base rate, cap, persistence and targeted faults together (Table~\ref{tab:profiles}), so the curves compare severity levels and do not isolate the effect of the base rate.
All four points of a curve come from the same \cmcaTrackSCurveTasks{} tasks, so they differ from the leaderboard columns, which cover every task, and they carry wider uncertainty ($n = 2$ on \code{light} and \code{heavy}).
On the service track, pass$^1$ under \code{heavy} ranges from \cmcaTrackSCodexClaudeCurveHeavy{} (Codex CLI) to \cmcaTrackSFxClaudeCurveHeavy{} (fx); on the native track from \cmcaTrackNFixedCcClaudeCurveHeavy{} (Claude Code) to \cmcaTrackNFixedFxhClaudeCurveHeavy{} (fxh).
Robustness AUC is in Table~\ref{tab:detail-s} and Table~\ref{tab:detail-n}.

\subsection{Fault delivery, exclusions and cost}

Table~\ref{tab:detail-s} and Table~\ref{tab:detail-n} count delivery over every trial run, including originals that a delivery rerun replaced; the lowest injection fidelity over verifiable faults is \cmcaTrackSFidelityMin{} on the service track and \cmcaTrackNFixedFidelityMin{} on the native track.
The run's stop rule required at least 99\% delivery per configuration; Codex CLI on the native track falls below it in these totals because they include the replaced originals, and its undelivered faults were rerun.
Three units have no counted trial because the original and its rerun both missed delivery: one Claude Code native unit (an output the harness saved to a file before the hook saw it, fixed after it was found), one Codex CLI native unit (Codex rejects a command after the injector has decided on it) and one OpenCode service unit (an \code{empty} fault on an error response shows as a tool error).
Cost is what each harness reports for the run, including reruns: a session total (Claude Code), a sum of per-response costs (Pi, OpenCode), settled gateway records (fx, fxh), or bridge usage priced at list prices (Codex CLI).
These sources are not provider billing and are not cross-checked against each other, so cost comparisons between rows are approximate.
