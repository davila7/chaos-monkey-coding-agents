The [previous post](/blog/what-each-new-agent-costs) ended with a question: which providers to fund, and how much on each, to take the benchmark from 12 tasks per track to 40. This post covers how that went. M6 is finished, the [results site](https://www.chaos-monkey.tech) reads it, and the data is public on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/davila7/chaos-monkey-coding-agents).

Every number here comes from the M6 stage reports and the release report (`reports/2026-11-18-m6-release.md`). Costs are harness-reported, not provider invoices.

## What we ran

Five agents, all on Claude Sonnet 5.5, on two tracks:

- **Track S** (tool calls to mock services over MCP): Claude Code, Codex, fx, OpenCode and Pi, on 37 tasks.
- **Track N** (a real repository, the agent's own shell and file tools): Claude Code, Codex, fxh (our fork of fx with tool hooks), OpenCode and Pi, on 36 tasks.

Each agent ran every task under the `clean` and `moderate` profiles, three attempts each. On top of that, a subset of 20 tasks per track ran under `light` and `heavy`, two attempts each, to draw a degradation curve from no faults to many. The release holds 3,011 trials over 73 tasks.

## How we ran it: four stages, one at a time

M6 was planned as about $670 on Anthropic and $275 on Vercel. Neither account had that at the start, so the run was split into stages (decision D-0052 set the rule): run one stage with the balance at hand, write its report, then top up and start the next.

| Stage | What | Trials | Anthropic | Vercel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1a | Calibration of new tasks and a cost gate | 180 | $28.24 | $8.31 |
| 1b | Track S leaderboard | 540 | $66.98 | $26.09 |
| 2 | Track N leaderboard | 510 | $109.55 | $52.63 |
| 3 | Calibration of 42 more tasks | 210 | $41.83 | |
| 3 | Leaderboard on the new tasks | 1,140 | $178.89 | $80.61 |
| 4 | `light` and `heavy` curves | 800 | $138.92 | $61.73 |

In total that is $564.41 on Anthropic and $229.37 on Vercel, under the plan on both. The stage 3 Anthropic figure includes $1.32 for jobs rerun after the disk filled.

## What went wrong

**Credit, every stage.** Anthropic and Vercel are separate prepaid balances, and from stage 1b on, every stage needed a top-up on at least one of them. Part of the Anthropic money was a promotional credit that expired on 2026-11-14; stages 1a and 1b ran on it before then. Stage 3 was split in two because the balances did not line up: the Vercel agents ran on 2026-11-16, and the four Anthropic agents waited until there was credit and ran on 2026-11-17. In stage 4 Vercel ran about 10% over its estimate per trial. The last two Vercel jobs had to wait for a $20 top-up, and the balance ended at $1.64.

**The disk filled.** At the start of the stage 3 Anthropic run, the container ran out of disk. Earlier Track N runs had left 4.7 GB of generated repositories in `/tmp`, and the Docker build cache held another 4.5 GB. Trials failed with `No space left on device`. Six jobs were set aside and rerun from scratch. Their measured cost was $1.32; the spend of the trials cut in flight is not measured. Two fixes came out of it: the plan command now removes its generated repositories when it exits, and the runner starts a job only when there is enough free disk.

**Containers that restarted.** The cloud container restarted under us once in stage 1b and three times in stage 4 (2026-11-18 at about 00:36, 03:28 and 04:40 UTC). Each restart killed Docker, the runner and every trial in flight. 17 trials were cut across the two stages; each was moved aside and its job resumed, which ran the missing trials from scratch. In stage 2 the shell running the jobs hit the session's time limit and cut two more. The spend of cut trials before the cut is not in any result file, so it is not counted above. While recovering in stage 4 we also found that the runner's skip check accepted a result whose `finished_at` was empty, which would have skipped two unfinished jobs. The continuation runner checks for it.

**Faults that did not reach the model.** The stop rule says fault delivery must be at least 99% on every agent. Stage 2 stopped on Claude Code at 90 of 91: when a command prints more than about 30 KB, Claude Code saves the output to a file and shows the model a blank preview, and our injector only replaced the preview. That was fixed (D-0054) before stage 3. Codex needed a change of its own (D-0053): a fault on a command that is still running when Codex stops waiting for it is now logged as not delivered instead of being counted. Two cases still repeat on rerun and are reported as exclusions: Codex rejecting a command after a fault was applied to it, and an `empty` fault on an MCP error response showing up as a tool error.

**Curves that mixed two task sets.** The first analysis of the full run drew each curve point over every task that profile had. `clean` and `moderate` cover all 73 tasks, `light` and `heavy` only the 20-task subset, so the curves mixed two task sets. The release now computes every curve point on the subset only (D-0055), and the site labels the curve with it.

**Seven tasks that did not pass calibration.** Before a task counts, Claude Code runs it five times with no faults and its pass rate has to sit inside a fixed band. Seven of the 56 tasks written for M6 missed it: `s-webhook-signature`, `s-price-revision`, `s-transit-close`, `n-plugin-registry`, `n-semver-ranges`, `n-cmake-build` and `n-scheduled-jobs`. They are in the reserve folder, not deleted, which is why M6 has 37 and 36 tasks instead of 40 and 40.

## Where the results are now

- **The site** reads the M6 release. The home opens with the ranking by tasks completed under faults, and the curves page shows the 20-task subset.
- **The dataset** is public on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/davila7/chaos-monkey-coding-agents): one row per trial (agent, task, profile, attempt, success, delivered faults, overclaim, cost and tokens), one row per task, the task files, the fault catalog and the profiles. The held-out tasks are not in it.
- **The paper** has its results sections written from the same release, and is in preparation.

## Next

The M6 runs are complete and nothing else is scheduled to spend. What comes next is the paper, and deciding what to do with the seven reserve tasks in the next version of the suite.
